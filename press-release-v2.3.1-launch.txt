SpecTool Brings Revit-Native CSI Spec Generation to the AV Vertical

New Pro add-in generates CSI MasterFormat Part 2 (Products) articles
directly from AV and security equipment in the Revit model — matched
to the consultant's own boilerplate voice.

WALHALLA, S.C. — July 22, 2026 — BIM Acoustics today released SpecTool,
a new Revit add-in that generates CSI MasterFormat Part 2 (Products)
specifications directly from the AV and security equipment placed in
a Revit model. SpecTool ships as Release Candidate 1 in AVTools System
Designer v2.3.1 Pro, covering CSI Divisions 11 (equipment), 27
(communications / audiovisual) and 28 (electronic safety and security).

Revit and specification integration is not a new category — Deltek
e-SPECS, RIB SpecLink, Deltek Specpoint, and Chalkline VisiSpecs have
served the architectural side for years — but none of those tools
target the AV vertical, ship with UFGS 27 41 00 and AVIXA standards
bundled, or generate spec language in the consultant's own firm voice
rather than pulling from a subscription library.

Rather than imposing a spec style, SpecTool respects three separate
authorities the consultant already answers to. The architect's project
spec template, uploaded once per project, supplies styles, fonts,
headers, footers, and page numbering — the generated .docx ends up
looking like every other section in the architect's book. The
consultant's own firm boilerplate, uploaded per section, supplies
Part 1 (General) and Part 3 (Execution) exactly as the firm writes
them. The Revit model itself is the product data source: the equipment
placed in the model determines what products land in Part 2, at what
quantities, from which manufacturers, at what performance parameters.

SpecTool fills in only Part 2, matched to the depth, voice, and field
selection of a Part 2 exemplar from the consultant's own boilerplate.
It does not invent products, override the architect's format, or
rewrite Part 1 or Part 3.

The wizard offers four detail levels for Part 2 generation: User
Boilerplate Detail (the default — mirrors the depth and voice of the
consultant's own articles), Basis of Design (open-proprietary CSI
convention with "Basis of Design: <manufacturer> <model>" openers and
"Substitutions per Section 01 25 00" closers), Minimal Spec (leanest
— Type + one PR2 sentence + Acceptable Products), and All Manufacturer
Specs (every performance value as a PR3 bullet). If a firm has no
boilerplate for a given section, SpecTool ships a bundled starter for
27 41 00 (Audio-Visual Systems) built from the verbatim NAVFAC UFGS
27 41 00 (February 2026) Part 1 and Part 3 plus a curated AVIXA
supplement (ANSI/AVIXA V201.01, V202.01, F202.01, and RP-C303.01).

Data confidentiality is enforced by a test harness that inspects every
outgoing prompt: project name, building name, owner, architect,
consultant, and any address or geographic identifier are never sent
to Anthropic's Claude API. Only generic content — section number,
article name, equipment list, and the consultant's boilerplate
exemplar — leaves the machine.

"Every Revit AV tool up to now — including mine, until this release —
has been about the drawings and the model. Layout, coverage, acoustics,
circuiting, cabling. All useful. But nobody has touched the other half
of the AV documentation deliverable — the spec itself — in a way that
respects how AV consultants actually work," said Jerrold Stevens,
founder of BIM Acoustics. "SpecTool doesn't ask consultants to adopt
a new spec library or change their voice. It reads the model they've
already built, respects the boilerplate they already wrote, and fills
in only Part 2. That's the whole idea."

SpecTool operates as a wizard on the BIM Acoustics ribbon in Revit
(BIM Acoustics → AV Tools → SpecTool) with five ordered steps plus a
Home screen, and supports both create and update workflows — first
issue creates a spec against the project template; subsequent issues
update the last-issued .docx in place, with tool-owned Part 2 articles
re-generated and hand-edited content elsewhere preserved.
